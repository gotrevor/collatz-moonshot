## 1. Lift prime cancellation to an actual path

**First experimental bet.**  The algebraic relaxation already has a powerful theorem.  Instead of inventing a new scalar potential, isolate exactly what must be added to that theorem to obtain a path.

Write `T(u)=(3u+1)/2` for odd positive `u`, and `r_u=u/T(u)`.  Applegate and Lagarias prove that the multiplicative semigroup generated by `2` and these `r_u` is precisely the positive rationals whose reduced denominator is not divisible by 3.  In particular every positive integer has a finite commutative certificate.  This is an established theorem, not a new Collatz proof: [The 3x+1 Semigroup](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0411140).

Here is the information it forgets.  Associate an actual edge `u -> T(u)` to each odd factor.  Label each factor `2` by an even edge `2v -> v`.  Let `B` be vertex incidence, with edge column `e_u-e_T(u)`.  Let `L` send a vertex `e_u` to the vector of prime valuations of `u`; in particular `L(e_1)=0`.  A certificate with nonnegative integer edge counts `c` satisfies

```
L B c = L e_n.
```

A finite flow from `n` to `1` requires the stronger equation

```
B c = e_n - e_1.
```

For this deterministic graph the latter equation really implies that the forward orbit of `n` reaches `1`.  Otherwise its forward-reachable set is closed under outgoing edges and omits `1`; summing the finite flow divergence over this set is nonpositive, whereas the right side sums to 1.  Thus a flow theorem would retain precisely the missing dynamical information.

The defect `d=Bc-(e_n-e_1)` lies in `ker L`.  The proposed object of study is a family of **prime-neutral exchanges**: integer changes `h` with `LBh=0`, preserving nonnegative factor counts, allowed to introduce new generator labels.  Can a parametric family of exchanges decrease a well-founded measure of the vertex defect until it vanishes?  This is a question about lifting the known prime-balance certificate to vertex balance.  The connection to integer kernels and Markov bases suggests tools; it supplies no termination theorem by itself.

### A small obstruction that any repair must handle

The certificate

```
2^3 (3/5)(5/8) = 3
```

is an actual reversed path `1,2,4,8,5,3`.  Squaring it gives

```
2^6 (3/5)^2(5/8)^2 = 9,
```

but **no ordering of those factors is a reversed Collatz path**.  Starting at 1, the first odd reverse edge must be `8 -> 5`; after that the only possible odd reverse edge is `5 -> 3`.  Doublings from 3 can reach neither 5 nor 8, leaving unusable odd factors.  Doubling at 5 instead likewise strands them.

An actual certificate for 9 is

```
2^7 (9/14)(7/11)(11/17)(17/26)(13/20)(5/8) = 9,
```

with reversed path `1,2,4,8,5,10,20,13,26,17,11,7,14,9`.  After cancellation the exchange is the exact identity

```
r_3^2 r_5 = 2 r_9 r_7 r_11 r_17 r_13 = 9/40.
```

This proves that reordering is insufficient.  Indeed some new odd label is necessary in any path to 9, whose last reverse edge is `14 -> 9`.  The displayed repair also increases the number of factors and the largest odd label.  Those quantities are not useful decreasing measures for this repair.

**Next bounded experiment:** take certificates produced by the constructive semigroup proof, retain their vertex defects, and search for a *parametric* exchange shared by a family of bad certificates.  Merely enumerating actual orbits and reporting their telescoping products does not count.  The deliverable would be one symbolic exchange with an explicitly decreasing defect measure on its stated domain, or an obstruction to that proposed measure.  No such exchange family is established here.

There is also a simple limit on finite templates.  A shortcut word acts affinely with slope `3^k/2^m`.  Two fixed words applied to `n` and `p*n` cannot coalesce for infinitely many `n` when `p` is a prime other than 2 or 3: equality of slopes would require `p=2^a 3^b` for integers `a,b`.  A finite list of bounded-length word pairs cannot avoid this argument.  Thus a uniform lift of the semigroup proof's other prime multipliers would have to be adaptive or unbounded, rather than a finite table of fixed coalescence words.

## 2. Transport all pairwise differences around a cycle

**A different arithmetic observable for the cycle front.**  Existing one-state product identities and unordered spacing bounds leave false candidates.  A cycle also permutes its entire set of pairwise differences.  That couples different states, their parity classes, and their divisibility.

For a primitive rational shortcut cycle with `m` distinct states, let `E` and `O` be its even- and odd-numerator states, with `|O|=k`.  The denominator is odd.  The absolute Vandermonde product is invariant under the cycle permutation.  Even-even differences scale by `1/2`; odd-odd differences scale by `3/2`; mixed differences change from `|o-e|` to `|3o+1-e|/2`.  Cancelling gives the exact identity

```
product_{e in E, o in O} |(3o+1-e)/(o-e)|
    = 2^(m(m-1)/2) / 3^(k(k-1)/2).
```

For an integer cycle all the differences are integers.  Their real sizes and their prime-adic clustering cannot vary independently: for every nonzero integer difference `d`, `v_p(d) log p <= log |d|`.  The possible direction is an inequality for the mixed products that uses their *jointly realizable* order and residue-cluster structure.  This is not a claim of a second independent word-admission congruence; the repo has already ruled that out.

The identity alone excludes nothing.  It holds for rational cycles and negative cycles too.  In particular these exact controls must remain visible:

| Map / word | States or distinguishing feature | Mixed product |
|---|---|---|
| `3n+1`, `10` | `1,2` | `2` |
| `3n+1`, `110` | `-5,-7,-10` | `8/3` |
| `3n+1`, `1100` | `5/7,11/7,20/7,10/7` | `64/3` |
| `3n+1`, `11111000` | Positive rational, minimum gap `69/13 > 1` | `2^28/3^10` |
| `5n+1`, `1110000` | `13,33,83,208,104,52,26` | `2^21/5^3` |

The fourth row kills the tempting assertion that every nontrivial positive rational cycle must have a pair closer than 1.  Its states are

```
(211,323,491,743,1121,1688,844,422)/13.
```

**Next bounded experiment:** derive a bound on the mixed product for one precisely specified combination of ordered gaps and residue clusters, then test it against the repo's rational false candidates and the `5n+1` cycle.  The bar is excluding a family that survives the existing necessary conditions, without assuming integrality again under another name.  A new word census or the displayed identity by itself would not clear that bar.

## 3. Positive fixed series, with the orbit-packing bound retained

**Highest-risk analytical bet.**  A generic spectral-gap proposal is already defeated by signed fixed functions.  The more specific question concerns the positive integral cone and the growth bound available for a divergent *integer* orbit.

For `F(z)=sum_{n>=1} a_n z^n`, let `P(z^n)=z^T(n)`.  Coefficientwise fixedness is

```
a_n = a_(2n) + [n == 2 mod 3] a_((2n-1)/3).
```

Analytically, in the unit disk,

```
P F(z) = (F(sqrt(z))+F(-sqrt(z)))/2
       + sqrt(z)(F(z^(3/2))-F(-z^(3/2)))/2.
```

Let `D_u(z)=sum_{j>=0} z^(2^j u)`.  For odd `u`, `D_(3u+1)-D_u` is a signed fixed function.  A divergent orbit starting at odd `n` instead gives the nonnegative fixed series

```
F_n(z) = D_n(z) + sum_{j>=1} z^(T^j(n)).
```

These constructions and the operator viewpoint are established: [Neklyudov, Functional analysis approach to the Collatz conjecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.11859v4).  They are not the novelty proposed here.

For divergence, the two supports in `F_n` are disjoint, and all coefficients are 0 or 1: an intersection with the dyadic ray would force a return to `n`, while a repeated orbit state would force a cycle.  The repo's [orbit-packing bound](RESEARCH-2026-09-22-packing-shadow.md) supplies some explicit `beta<1` such that

```
sum_{n<=X} a_n = O(X^beta log(2+X)).
```

The dyadic ray adds only `O(log X)` terms.  Thus a candidate rigidity statement is:

> A nonnegative integer fixed series, whose nonzero support is connected in the undirected Collatz graph and has a sublinear power counting bound, has finite support.

Connectedness avoids asking for control over arbitrary unions of different cycles.  This is an open target, not a result: its application to `F_n` would exclude divergence, so it carries that difficulty.  The proposed research is to obtain a quantitative inequality from coefficient positivity and the exact functional equation that conflicts with the packing bound.  Calling the target a fixed-point theorem does not advance it.

Two controls prevent weakening away the hard part:

* `D_10-D_3` is fixed and logarithmically sparse, but signed.  Sparsity alone fails.
* For the **different** map `G(n)=n/2` on evens and `G(n)=3n` on odds, the series with support `{2^j:j>=0} union {3^j:j>=1}` is positive, 0/1, connected, logarithmically sparse, infinitely supported, and fixed.  Positivity, sparsity, and multiplicative independence alone fail too.  An argument must use the actual `+1` functional equation.

**Next bounded experiment:** derive a positive-coefficient inequality that couples more than the scalar count, for example several residue filters of the displayed functional equation, and evaluate it on these controls before attempting an infinite limit.  The existing scalar self-packing and phase-averaging obstructions remain applicable to any reduction that forgets these correlations.  There is no justified spectral gap or such inequality in hand yet.

## Assessment and scope

My first choice is the semigroup lift (65% confidence as a choice of next experiment, not as a probability of solving Collatz).  It starts from a strong existing theorem, exposes an exact lost datum, and has a tiny obstruction on which to test repairs.  Pairwise cycle transport is the second bet; positive fixed-series rigidity is the third because the analytical obstruction is less localized.

The proposal-specific formulations were checked against the repo's negative inventory.  None is claimed to be new in the literature.  The scripts provide exact finite controls and an exhaustive factor-ordering decision for a supplied finite certificate; they do not prove the general candidate statements.  Eleven CLI tests use independently computed anchors.  Their outputs are recorded in `experiments/research_lifts_controls.json`.
