## Problem

Klamkin (a) for `U(2n) = 3n, U(4n+1) = 3n+1, U(4n+3) = 3n+2` on `ℕ`: infinitely many `U`-orbits?

## What round 4 taught

Round 4 (`tmp/ideation-context/newroutes4-astra.md`, research §50) built faithful dictionaries to
foreign worlds: an itinerary module over `F₃[t, t⁻¹]`, a pants cover, a lamplighter group, a Lie
algebra, a ground-state space.  Every far-side target turned out **equivalent to (a)** once the
dictionary's kernel was understood (`pcancel.md`: the hull is (cycle torsion) ⊕ free of rank = number of
infinite orbits).  The far sides had tools, but none of their tools *saw* the one thing that separates
`U` from its foils: **integrality along whole tails** (the masquerade is `U`'s real-affine law on
non-integers; `F_L` and π are integer but differ on infinitely many blocks; §47).

So this round's leap has one extra requirement: **the far side's machinery must consume integrality**
(or `S`-integrality, or `ℤ` as a discrete subgroup of the adeles) as a hypothesis with teeth, and its
conclusion must be a finiteness or counting statement.  Langlands-scale is still the ambition: a
dictionary to a world where the counterpart of (a) is natural.

Seeds (not limits; go beyond them):
* **Integral points in orbits.**  Silverman's theorem: an orbit of a non-polynomial rational map
  contains finitely many integers.  `U` is piecewise `x ↦ 3x/2, (3x±1)/4`; its integer orbits are the
  objects.  Is there a Silverman-type statement (Siegel / Thue–Mahler / S-unit equations) for orbits of
  a piecewise-affine *correspondence* that counts something orbit-separating?  The ray identity
  `C_w/2^s = 2^B − 3^t` (Fable r3: only `8 ~ 32`, `16 ~ 512`) is an S-unit equation.
* **Adelic geometry.**  `ℤ` sits diagonally and discretely in `ℝ × ℚ₂ × ℚ₃` (or `ℝ × ℤ₂ × ℤ₃`); `U`
  extends to each factor (real-affine `Û`; on `ℤ₂ × ℤ₃` it is conjugate to the full 3-shift).  Integer
  orbits are the orbits that stay on the diagonal lattice.  Strong approximation, lattice point
  counting in homogeneous spaces (Eskin–McMullen, Duke–Rudnick–Sarnak), Ratner-type rigidity, or
  equidistribution of the diagonal.  What does "infinitely many diagonal orbits" become?
* **p-adic interpolation.**  Skolem–Mahler–Lech and Bell–Ghioca–Tucker interpolate integer orbits by
  p-adic analytic functions; zero sets are then finite unions of progressions.  Is there a 2-adic or
  3-adic analytic interpolation of `U`-orbits along a fixed branch word, and a finiteness theorem it
  feeds?
* **o-minimal counting** (Pila–Wilkie): integer points on definable sets are few unless the set has
  algebraic pieces.  Is some orbit-generated set definable in `ℝ_exp` (with `2^x`, `3^y`)?
* **Heights.**  Canonical heights for piecewise-affine dynamics; integrality as `h_∞ = h` and local
  heights at 2 and 3.
