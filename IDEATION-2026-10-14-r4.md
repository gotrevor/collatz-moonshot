## Problem

Klamkin (a) for Conway's amusical permutation `U(2n) = 3n, U(4n+1) = 3n+1, U(4n+3) = 3n+2` on `ℕ`:
does `ℕ` split into infinitely many `U`-orbits?

## Why this round is different

Rounds 1–3 produced ~120 candidates, and every one died at the same small boundary: residues,
densities, near-cycles, survivors, block swaps, random perturbations, finite operator certificates.
Trevor (2026-10-14): *"Feels like the boundary is small.  Think outside the box: radical new ideas not
connected directly to any of the closed-off paths.  Consider Langlands-level thinking leap."*

**So the target is a leap, not a repair.**  A Langlands-type move does not attack a hard statement
where it lives.  It builds a **dictionary** to a different world, where the statement's counterpart
is natural, structural, or already known, and where the other side has tools that do not exist here.
Examples of the shape: Fermat ↔ modularity of elliptic curves; class field theory ↔ characters of
idele class groups; Weil's point counts ↔ cohomology and a trace formula; Furstenberg's
Szemerédi ↔ multiple recurrence; Matui's groupoid homology ↔ topological full groups.

## What to produce

1. **Breadth of genuinely foreign worlds (≥ 10 candidates, ≥ 6 fields).**  For each: the world, the
   object `U` becomes there, and what "infinitely many orbits" becomes.  A candidate whose
   translation of (a) is a statement about residues, densities, survivors, near-cycles, swaps or
   finite commutants is **not** foreign; drop it.  Read the closed paths (below) to know where not to
   land, but do not let them shape generation.
2. **Two deep leaps.**  Pick the two dictionaries with the most structure on the far side.  For each:
   * the dictionary, made precise: objects, how `U` and its orbits translate, and at least one
     **proved dictionary entry** (a small theorem saying the translation is faithful somewhere);
   * the far-side statement equivalent to (or implying) (a), and what far-side machinery could
     touch it;
   * a **prediction** the dictionary makes that you can check by computation, with a known-answer
     control (e.g. the dictionary applied to a map whose orbit count is known: `x ↦ x+1` on ℤ, a
     finite-orbit foil, `U`'s 4-cycle and 2-cycle family, `U + 6^N`);
   * an honest foil check at the end: does the far-side statement distinguish `U` from the masquerade
     (real `Û`-orbit), `F_L` block-swap maps, and the finite-orbit foils?  A leap may legitimately
     land somewhere the foils do not exist; say why.
3. **Success is a dictionary with a checked prediction**, even if (a) stays open.  Low odds for (a)
   are fine; say them (0–100%).

Seeds, deliberately far from the closed paths.  Not limits; beat them if you can.
* **Thompson-like groups.**  `U` is a piecewise-affine permutation with slopes `3/2, 3/4` and
  breakpoints on residue classes: an element of a Stein / Brin–Higman–Thompson-type group acting on
  `ℤ` (or on `ℤ₂ × ℤ₃` with the adelic picture).  Conjugacy and dynamics of elements in these groups
  are classified by strand diagrams and revealing pairs (Brin, Belk–Matucci, Bleak et al.).  What
  does that machinery say about orbit counts of one element on a *discrete* invariant set?
* **Groupoids and homology.**  The transformation groupoid of `U`, or of the ample groupoid it
  lives in; Matui's HK conjecture; `H₀` as coinvariants counts orbits in the right setting.  Cuntz–Li
  computed K-theory of the `ax+b` ring algebra that §48's Theorem Z lives in.
* **Buildings and lattices.**  Slopes `2^a 3^b` suggest an action on the product of the Bruhat–Tits
  trees of `SL₂(ℚ₂)` and `SL₂(ℚ₃)`; `ℤ[1/6]`-points and S-arithmetic lattices.  Can `U` be read as a
  Hecke correspondence, or orbits as double cosets?
* **Zeta functions and trace formulas.**  Artin–Mazur zeta of `U` restricted to bounded windows, or a
  Lefschetz/trace formula for a transfer operator whose spectral side counts orbits.
* **Logic and model theory.**  The structure `(ℕ, +, U)` or `(ℤ, U)`: decidability, definability of
  orbit equivalence, o-minimal or NIP-type tameness, independence of (a) from weak arithmetic.
* **Symbolic dynamics, tilings, cellular automata, physics.**  Orbits as tiles of a substitution,
  a zero-temperature limit, a conserved quantity of an integrable system.
* Anything else: knot/braid encodings, categorification, motives, quantum groups.  Surprise us.
