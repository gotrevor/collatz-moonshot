graphs, itinerary modules, pants covers, S-unit lattices, Mahler numbers, `p`-adic interpolation).
Two lessons:
1. A dictionary whose tools ignore integrality lands on the *generic* fibre: (a) lives at the single
   fully ramified point (`ℤ` inside the `ax + b` groupoid on `ℤ₂ × ℤ₃`), and Theorem N (proved: no mean
   on `ℤ` is invariant under `n ↦ n+1` and `U`) shows averaging cannot see it.
2. A dictionary whose tools consume integrality (S-units, Baker, Ellison, Bennett, Mahler) sees
   **closed** Diophantine equations, i.e. cycles and joins.  An **infinite orbit is an open equation**:
   no finite word closes it, so every finiteness theorem stays silent about it.

## This round's target

**Find a world in which an infinite integer orbit is a closed, finite, or compact object**, so that
integrality-consuming finiteness theorems can count it.  Then the counterpart of (a) is "infinitely
many such closed objects", and the far side's tools may produce them.

Shapes to aim for (not limits):
* **Ends as points.**  An infinite orbit has two ends.  Is there a compactification (of `ℕ`, of the
  Schreier graph of `⟨n+1, U⟩`, of `ℤ[1/6]`) where each infinite orbit's ends are *points* with
  arithmetic structure (e.g. limits in `ℤ₂ × ℤ₃`, a boundary of a building, a solenoid point), and a
  theorem that integer orbits have distinct, isolated or algebraic ends?
* **Renormalization.**  An induced map (first return to a set, an accelerated map, a quotient by a
  conjugacy) under which an infinite orbit becomes periodic or eventually periodic, with the
  renormalized system still integral.  §38 C1 and the ray joins (§52) are local versions.
* **Duality.**  A transform under which open and closed swap: generating functions (Pólya–Carlson,
  §52: "exactly one infinite orbit ⟺ `Σ_{n∈O(8)} 2^{−n} ∈ ℚ`"), Fourier on the solenoid, a Galois
  correspondence, Pontryagin or Stone duality, Koszul duality.
* **Limits of cycles.**  Infinite orbits as limits of cycles of nearby integral maps (`U + 6^N` on `ℤ`,
  `U` mod `6^N`, finite permutations `U_N` on `ℤ/6^Nℤ`), with a semicontinuity theorem for orbit counts
  that the integer structure forces.
* **Categorify.**  Replace orbits by objects of a category where infinite orbits are compact objects or
  have finite length.
