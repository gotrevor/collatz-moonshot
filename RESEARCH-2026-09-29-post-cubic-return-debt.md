# Post-cubic debt: what smaller-endpoint paths can discharge

This is a paper audit of the hard K64 subclass in `RESEARCH-2026-09-29-head-continuation.md`.  It uses no trajectory of the target `n` and supplies no completed repair block.  Its useful correction is that a proof-specific repair could end with a **finite signed edge flow** of the exact target boundary; nonnegative flow and return of an identical borrowed unit are sufficient, but not necessary, for that particular endpoint theorem.

## Exact residual

Write `T(u)=(3u+1)/2` for odd `u`, `T(u)=u/2` for even `u`, `r_u=u/T(u)` and `B_u=e_u-e_(T(u))`.  On the K64 progression,

```
n = 25542881863 + 39026668800s,
m = 17959838810 + 27440626500s,
a = T(n) = 38314322795 + 58540003200s,
v = T(5n) = 191571613973 + 292700016000s,
T(v)=16m,
T(a)=57471484193+87810004800s,
5T(a)=16m+5.
```

The two Q peels and the variable cubic give the **central vertex residual**

```
D = e_(16m)-e_(T(a))
    + B_d+B_b+B_e1+B_e2-B_x-B_z-B_q1-B_q2.       (1)
```

Every one of `d,b,e1,e2,x,z,q1,q2` is a positive odd label strictly below `m`, as checked symbolically in the continuation note.  Also `m<n<T(a)`: the first inequality follows from the displayed affine coefficients, and the second has exact difference `T(a)-n=31928602330+48783336000s>0`.

## Signed edge flow is enough, if its *vertex* boundary is exact

For a finitely supported integer assignment `F(u)` to actual edges `u→T(u)`, put `∂F=Σ_u F(u)(e_u-e_(T(u)))`.  Then, for every `n`,

> There exists finite signed `F` with `∂F=e_n-e_1` **if and only if** some forward iterate of `n` is `1`.

The forward path gives the reverse implication.  For the other direction let `χ(u)=1` if the forward orbit of `u` reaches 1 and `χ(u)=0` otherwise.  Determinism gives `χ(u)=χ(T(u))` on every edge, regardless of its coefficient in `F`.  Pairing with the finite boundary gives `χ(n)-χ(1)=Σ_u F(u)(χ(u)-χ(T(u)))=0`, hence `χ(n)=1`.  Equivalently, a finite signed boundary joins `n` and 1 in the undirected graph of a functional digraph, and their forward orbits coalesce.  This criterion is mathematically exact but not an easier existence theorem: constructing that boundary is already enough to settle this `n`.

The scalar invariant is weaker.  The genuine Q identity `r_11 r_17=r_7 r_121=11/26` has left boundary `B_11+B_17=e_11-e_26`, whereas its right boundary is `B_7+B_121=e_7-e_11+e_121-e_182`.  In particular the coefficient of `e_121` differs.  So a scalar move is not a zero-boundary move, even when both sides are positive legal words.  A value-one borrowed word likewise need not have zero boundary, and a bare power of 2 does not specify the source of an even edge.

## Applying the smaller-input induction hypothesis

Assume strong induction supplies an actual finite forward path `P_u` from every `u<m` to 1, and `P_m` from `m` to 1.  Each `u` in the lower part of (1) has such a path.  Its first edge is `B_u`, and writing its tail as `Q_u` gives

```
∂P_u=e_u-e_1,
∂Q_u=e_(T(u))-e_1,
B_u=∂(P_u-Q_u).                                    (2)
```

This identity is valid as an integer signed edge chain, though `P_u-Q_u` simplifies back to the single edge at `u`; it creates no new bridge.  Let `E_m` be the four actual even edges `16m→8m→4m→2m→m`.  Then `∂(E_m+P_m)=e_(16m)-e_1`.  With `L={d,b,e1,e2}` and `R={x,z,q1,q2}`, equation (1) becomes

```
D = ∂[E_m+P_m + Σ_(u∈L)(P_u-Q_u)
                 - Σ_(u∈R)(P_u-Q_u)]
    + e_1-e_(T(a)).                                (3)
```

All edges in the bracket are already in the known basin of 1.  Equivalently, if `H` is generated by `e_w-e_1` for vertices `w` on these supplied paths and the four even edges, then

```
D ≡ e_1-e_(T(a))  (mod H).                          (4)
```

If `T(a)` happens to lie on one of those supplied paths, that fact itself proves this target case, but no such intersection is supplied by the scalar identities or by the IH alone.  Otherwise (4) leaves exactly the actual second frontier.  Adding or subtracting only the available IH paths changes the bracket in (3), not the residual class.  An additional finite signed chain whose boundary is `e_(T(a))-e_1` would close it; by the signed-flow criterion, such a chain already proves `T(a)` converges.  This is why invoking the auxiliary IH paths as a generic “return” would be circular.

## What the cubic's multiplier pattern actually gives

The cubic labels have several exact scaled-successor relations, all by substitution into `T`:

```
T(a)=29e1,       m=10q1,
T(e2)=a/7,       T(q2)=v/31,
32T(q1)=29T(e1).                                    (5)
```

There is a real basin consequence here.  Since `q1<m`, IH gives a path from `T(q1)` to 1.  The equality `29T(e1)=32T(q1)` and five even steps therefore give a path from `29T(e1)` to 1.  But `e1` is odd and `T(a)=29e1` is odd, so the actual next edge lands at

```
T(T(a)) = T(29e1) = 29T(e1)-14 = 32T(q1)-14.      (6)
```

It is **14 below** a known-basin vertex, not equal to it.  This is the general odd-multiplier commutator `T(ku)=kT(u)-(k-1)/2` for odd `k,u`; Collatz orbits have no translation-invariance that erases the carry.  Likewise `T(e2)=a/7` gives `T(a)=7T(T(e2))-3` (the displayed progression makes `T(e2)` odd), but IH for `e2` does not put its sevenfold multiple in the basin.  The relation `m=10q1` gives a particular known-basin multiple `5q1` after one even step, not a uniform multiplier rule.

The pair loses its small offset immediately under lockstep iteration.  Put `q=T(q1)`, `A=T^3(n)=32q-14`, and `R=32q`, where `R` is in the known basin.  Both `A,R` are even, so their next pair is `(16q-7,16q)`, at distance 7.  Now their parities differ.  The next pair is

```
(T(16q-7), T(16q)) = (24q-10, 8q),
gap = 16q-10.                                      (7)
```

Here `q=(3q1+1)/2>0`, so the gap grows affinely with the family parameter, rather than remaining a bounded carry.  The actual third step after (6) is `T^6(n)=12q-5=18q1+1`; this is the fixed six-step prefix, not a borrowed target trajectory.  `DIRECTION.md` has already retired a run-merging descent because its offset changes by a large arithmetic term; (7) shows the analogous issue for this particular paired basin neighbor.  It does not exclude a richer coupled state.

Thus a proposed rank on a smaller base label plus a multiplier has a precise missing transition: it must handle the additive carry in (6) and the parity split in (7), then turn a known-basin vertex into the actual frontier.  Counting `e1,q1,e2,q2<m` as a rank decrease while discarding multipliers would assume that transition.  The identities in (5) suggest a constrained multiplier state, but do not close it.

## Separate the two debts

* **Factor/borrowing debt:** the central scalar peels consume `x,z,q1,q2` and produce `d,b,e1,e2` (besides changing the virtual and actual head factors).  Unrestricted unit supply permits those factors to be borrowed, but their identical return has not been shown.  A completed nonnegative borrow-use-return block would settle this debt and control its boundary.
* **Vertex/edge debt:** after every lower-label path and the virtual suffix is accounted for, (3) leaves `e_1-e_(T(a))`.  This debt is invisible to scalar equality and is not discharged merely by representing inverses of factors as value-one words.  A proof-specific signed-flow construction could bypass identical unit return, but it must still produce the exact full vertex boundary.

Thus the post-cubic calculation gives a second local peel and an exact normal form for what remains.  It does **not** provide a nonnegative completed block, a signed target-boundary flow, or a decreasing rank for full repair.  The unresolved part is the actual frontier `T(a)>n`, not the eight lower auxiliary starts.
