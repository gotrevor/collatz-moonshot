# Negative rational shadows: an exact potential, and two obstructions

Research pass requested by Trevor: find a direction that changes the mathematical mechanism, rather than extend the existing few-run, packing, or first-crossing ladders.  Repo inspected at `c6bcd22`.  This note records a tested candidate and its failure, not a new route to a Collatz proof.  No proof campaign was launched.

## Verdict

The candidate uses the distance from a positive integer to a negative rational reference in both the ordinary and 2-adic senses.  It has a useful exact transport identity.  The difficulty is selecting references across changes of parity.

- The maximum over a finite catalog of negative rational cycles is not a Lyapunov function.  At catalog depth 10 there are 387 references and 253 increases for starts 3 through 1000; the first is `6 -> 3`, with values `7 -> 16`.
- Enlarging to **all** primitive negative rationals makes the supremum exactly `(n+1)^2`.  This loses the proposed arithmetic advantage; the result below is an elementary proof, not an inference from the scan.
- Closing a reference family under inverse branches introduces factors of 3 in denominators.  The natural denominator weight restores transport but makes the supremum infinite already at `n=1`.  The ordinary positive cycle supplies explicit witnesses.

These conclusions retire the particular envelopes below.  They do not rule out every use of negative references or prove a general impossibility theorem for arithmetic Lyapunov functions.  There is currently no surviving bounded statement here that clears the repo's no-proof-lap-without-a-mechanism gate.

## 1. Candidate and exact transport

Use the shortcut map `T(n)=n/2` for even n and `(3n+1)/2` for odd n.  Let `r=-a/b <= -1`, with `a,b` positive, `b` odd, and `gcd(a,b)=1`.  Define

```
q = bn+a,
S_r(n) = q * 2^v_2(q) / a^2.
```

For every positive integer n,

```
S_r(n) <= q^2/a^2 <= (n+1)^2.
```

The first inequality is divisibility: `2^v_2(q) <= q`.  The second uses `b <= a`.  Thus even an infinite supremum of these unweighted scores is finite without a Collatz assumption.

When n and r have the same parity, transport the reference by the same branch.  First assume `3` does not divide b, as for every periodic rational reference generated by a parity word.

On an even step, `a'=a/2`, `b'=b`, `q'=q/2`, and

```
S_{T(r)}(T(n)) = S_r(n).
```

On an odd step, `a'=(3a-b)/2`, `b'=b`, `q'=3q/2`, and

```
S_{T(r)}(T(n)) / S_r(n) = 3a^2/(3a-b)^2 <= 3/4.
```

Here `a>=b` ensures `a'>=b`, so the reference stays at or below -1.  For an even step one must separately require `a/2>=b`; this holds on a negative periodic orbit.  A periodic negative orbit cannot enter `(-1,0)`: both branches strictly increase there while negative, and a point that becomes nonnegative cannot return to the negative orbit.

This is the attractive part of the construction: actual odd-step expansion is charged against 2-adic agreement with an expanding negative reference.  For `r=-1`, it reduces to

```
H(n) = (n+1) * 2^v_2(n+1),
H(T(n)) = (3/4) H(n)  whenever n is odd.
```

Even steps can replenish H: `H(26)=27`, but `H(13)=28`.  Introducing `r=-2` pays for that particular even step: both corresponding transported scores are 28.  This is a useful diagnostic, not a global repair.

## 2. Why the maximum over periodic references does not inherit transport

For a parity word of length m with K odd letters, write

```
T_word(x) = (3^K x + N)/2^m.
```

If `3^K>2^m`, its fixed point is the negative rational

```
r = -N/(3^K-2^m).
```

The probe enumerates and reduces these fractions for all words through a specified depth.  They are actual periodic rational references; the word's parity congruences hold at its fixed point.

Following one compatible reference gives the identities above.  Maximizing independently at the next integer does not guarantee that the new maximizer has a compatible predecessor in the catalog.  At depth 10 the first failures are

| n | T(n) | maximum before | maximum after |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 3 | 7 | 16 |
| 10 | 5 | 11 | 12 |
| 14 | 7 | 15 | 64 |
| 26 | 13 | 27 | 28 |

This finite scan refutes that specific catalog, not every larger periodic catalog.

## 3. All-rational completion collapses exactly

**Proposition.**  For every integer `n>=1`,

```
sup_{a>=b>=1, b odd, gcd(a,b)=1} S_{-a/b}(n) = (n+1)^2.
```

The upper bound was proved in section 1.  For the lower bound take `q=2^k`, let b be the largest positive odd integer at most `q/(n+1)`, and set `a=q-bn`.  For sufficiently large k these are admissible: `a>=b`, and `gcd(a,b)=gcd(q,b)=1`.  Moreover

```
0 <= q-(n+1)b < 2(n+1),
a/b -> 1,
bn+a=q=2^k,
S_{-a/b}(n) = q^2/a^2 -> (n+1)^2.
```

Thus the supremum is explicitly known and increases on every positive odd shortcut step.  Restricting to a genuine arithmetic subfamily is indispensable; finiteness of the all-rational envelope alone buys nothing.

## 4. The denominator correction is already infinite on the trivial cycle

Taking the odd inverse of a destination reference gives

```
r'=-a/b  ->  r=-(2a+b)/(3b).
```

Unless the numerator cancels a factor 3, the source denominator grows.  The earlier odd transport law assumed `3` did not divide the denominator.  Ignoring this is a mathematical error, not a small loss in a bound.  For example,

```
9 -> 14,        -5/3 -> -2,
S_{-5/3}(9)=1024/25,   S_{-2}(14)=64.
```

The ratio is `25/16>1`.

Consider the inverse basin of -1.  Its reduced denominators are powers of 3.  The modified score

```
W_{-a/b}(n) = b * S_{-a/b}(n)
```

does restore compatible-branch transport: even steps preserve it and odd steps multiply it by at most `3/4`.  When `3|b`, reduction gives `a'=(3a-b)/6`, `b'=b/3`, `q'=q/2`; multiplying by b cancels the extra factor 3.  When `b=1`, the preceding calculation applies directly.  All inverse images of -1 lie at or below -1.

Unfortunately its supremum over that inverse basin is infinite even at 1.  Take the inverse image of -1 along p repetitions of the positive cycle word `(odd,even)`:

```
a_p = 2*4^p - 3^p,
b_p = 3^p,
r_p = -a_p/b_p.
```

These fractions are primitive; applying the word gives -1.  At `n=1`, `b_p+a_p=2*4^p`, hence

```
W_{r_p}(1) = 3^p * (2*4^p)^2/(2*4^p-3^p)^2 > 3^p.
```

The first values are `4`, `192/25`, `9216/529`.  Thus reference closure plus this weight does not produce a finite potential.  It fails on a known convergent orbit, without any hypothetical counterexample.

## 5. Novelty boundary and next gate

Negative rational cycles, 2-adic shadowing, and obstructions to finite-residue Lyapunov functions are established themes.  This note makes no literature-priority claim.  Its specific contribution to this repo is the explicit score, the all-rational supremum calculation, and the denominator-weight counterexample, with a reproducible falsification instrument.

Other mechanisms considered in this research pass do not yet supply a replacement: the orbit's real shadow constant has no proved algebraicity; the repo's harmonic certificate already defeats the proposed bounded inverse-tree correlation repair; an unconstrained spectral-gap pitch must account for known infinite families of signed fixed functions (see Neklyudov, [Functional analysis approach to the Collatz conjecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.11859v4)).  These are reasons to demand an actual new inequality, not recommendations to rename the existing gaps.

A future negative-reference proposal must specify its admissible family, why its score is finite, how it handles a change of maximizing reference, and why it survives both the `9 -> 14` denominator example and the `(1,2)` cycle witnesses.  Merely saying “track switching costs” does not clear that gate.

Both general propositions above are now machine-checked in
`CollatzMoonshot/Obstructions/NegativeShadow.lean`, imported from the root
build.  `score_le_square` and `scoreValues_isLUB` give section 3 exactly: the
all-rational envelope equals `(n+1)^2` as a rational least upper bound, with no
real-supremum machinery, no restriction on `n`, and the actual score rather than
its upper bound at the sharpness end.  `envelope_not_nonincreasing` turns that
into the refutation of the proposed monotonicity, using `tstep 3 = 5` and
`36 > 16`.  Section 4 is covered by `witness_admissible`,
`witness_reaches_neg_one`, `witness_weighted_gt`,
`weighted_witnesses_unbounded` and `inverse_basin_scores_unbounded`; the
dynamics half is proved through the reduced numerator (`Rat.num` via
`Rat.num_div_eq_of_coprime`), so the rational parity test is genuinely checked
and not assumed, and the closed pair map factors as
`witnessRef (p+1) -> 2 * witnessRef p -> witnessRef p`.  The finite depth-10
scan of section 2 is pinned by `catalog_ten_six`, `catalog_ten_three` and
`catalog_ten_increases`.

Axiom audit: every headline declaration depends only on `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, with no `sorryAx`.  The three depth-10
catalog facts additionally carry their own `native_decide` axioms, which is the
pinned evaluation convention for that finite scan; nothing else in the module
uses `native_decide`, including the weighted anchors `4`, `192/25`, `9216/529`,
which are derived from the closed form in the kernel.
